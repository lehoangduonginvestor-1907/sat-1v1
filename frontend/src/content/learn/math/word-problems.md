Word problems appear in nearly every Math skill. The math inside is usually simple; the challenge is turning sentences into equations without misreading. This lesson is about a dependable translation process.

## Translate piece by piece

| Words | Math |
| --- | --- |
| is, was, will be, equals | $=$ |
| of (with fractions/percents) | $\times$ |
| more than, increased by, sum | $+$ |
| less than, decreased by, difference | $-$ |
| per, for each, ratio of | $\div$ or a rate |
| twice, triple | $2\times$, $3\times$ |

:::trap "Less than" reverses the order
"5 less than $x$" is $x - 5$, not $5 - x$. "A number is 3 more than twice another" is $a = 2b + 3$.
:::

## A four-step process

:::steps
1. **Define variables** clearly, with units: "$c$ = number of children's tickets."
2. **Write one equation per fact** in the problem. Two unknowns usually need two equations.
3. **Solve**, by hand or in Desmos.
4. **Answer the actual question**: the problem may ask for the total, the difference, or a different variable from the one you solved for.
:::

:::examples
✓ A theater sold 240 tickets. Adult tickets cost \$12 and child tickets \$7, and sales totaled \$2,280. Let $a$ = adult tickets, $c$ = child tickets: $a + c = 240$ and $12a + 7c = 2{,}280$. Solving gives $a = 120$, $c = 120$.
:::

## Common question types

- **Fixed cost + rate**: total $= \text{fixed} + \text{rate} \times \text{quantity}$ (phone plans, taxis, rentals).
- **Comparing plans**: set the two expressions equal to find when they cost the same; then test a value to see which is cheaper after that point.
- **Consecutive or related quantities**: "Sam is 4 years older than twice Ana's age."
- **"Which equation models…"**: you don't need to solve at all. Check each choice against the facts, including units.

### Choosing the variable

- Let the variable stand for **exactly what the question asks for**, so you can't forget a final conversion step.
- Use as few variables as possible: if Lena is 6 years older than Omar, call Omar $x$ and Lena $x + 6$ instead of using two letters.
- Consecutive integers: $x, x + 1, x + 2$. Consecutive even (or odd) integers: $x, x + 2, x + 4$.

### Three classic setups

- **Distance**: $d = rt$. Split a trip into legs and add: $d = r_1t_1 + r_2t_2$. When one traveler catches another, their distances are equal.
- **Mixtures**: adding a substance changes **both** the amount of that substance and the total amount. Mixing $x$ liters of pure juice into 10 liters of a drink that is 20% juice to reach 50%: $\dfrac{0.2(10) + x}{10 + x} = 0.5$, so $x = 6$.
- **Work**: rates add, times don't. If one pump fills a tank in 3 hours and another in 6, together they fill $\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{6} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ tank per hour, so 2 hours.

:::tip Use the choices
When choices are numbers, you can test them in the conditions of the problem (back-solving). Start with a middle value so you know whether to go higher or lower.
:::

## Checking your answer

Plug your result back into the original words, not your equation. If you misread a fact, your equation will agree with your answer while the story won't. Also ask whether the answer is reasonable: a negative number of people or a fraction of a bus means something went wrong (or the question wants you to round in a specific direction).

:::check
A gym charges a \$45 sign-up fee plus \$22 per month. Which expression gives the total cost, in dollars, for $m$ months?
- [ ] $45m + 22$
- [x] $22m + 45$
- [ ] $67m$
- [ ] $45 + 22 + m$
> The monthly charge multiplies the number of months; the fee is added once.
:::

:::check
The sum of two numbers is 42, and the larger number is 6 less than twice the smaller number. What is the larger number?
- [ ] 16
- [x] 26
- [ ] 28
- [ ] 36
> Let $s$ = smaller, $L$ = larger: $s + L = 42$ and $L = 2s - 6$. Substitute: $3s - 6 = 42$, so $s = 16$ and $L = 26$.
:::

:::check
Plan A costs \$30 per month plus \$0.10 per text. Plan B costs \$45 per month with unlimited texts. For how many texts per month do the plans cost the same?
- [ ] 15
- [ ] 75
- [x] 150
- [ ] 450
> $30 + 0.10t = 45 \Rightarrow 0.10t = 15 \Rightarrow t = 150$.
:::

## Summary

- Translate phrase by phrase; "less than" flips the order.
- Define variables with units, write one equation per fact, then answer the question that was asked.
- For "which equation models" questions, test the choices against the facts.
- Check your answer against the original words.
