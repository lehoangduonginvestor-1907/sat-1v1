Radians are another unit for measuring angles. They appear in circle and trigonometry questions, and converting between radians and degrees is a quick, guaranteed point once you know one fact.

## The key fact

:::rule Degrees and radians
$$180^\circ = \pi \text{ radians}$$
- Degrees → radians: multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$.
- Radians → degrees: multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$.
:::

:::examples
✓ $45^\circ \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians.
✓ $\dfrac{5\pi}{6} \times \dfrac{180}{\pi} = 150^\circ$.
:::

## Common angles

| Degrees | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | $270^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Radians | $\tfrac{\pi}{6}$ | $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$ | $\tfrac{\pi}{3}$ | $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ | $\pi$ | $\tfrac{3\pi}{2}$ | $2\pi$ |

## What a radian means

One radian is the angle that cuts off an arc **equal in length to the radius**. That's why the arc-length formula is so simple in radians:

$$s = r\theta \qquad \text{(θ in radians)}$$

A full circle has circumference $2\pi r$, which is $2\pi$ radius-lengths, so a full turn is $2\pi$ radians.

:::examples
✓ In a circle of radius 5, a central angle of $\tfrac{2\pi}{5}$ radians cuts off an arc of length $5 \cdot \tfrac{2\pi}{5} = 2\pi$.
:::

The sector area formula also simplifies: $A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.

## Slopes and angles

A line through the origin with slope $m$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the positive $x$-axis where $\tan\theta = m$. Slope $1$ means $45^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{4}$; slope $\sqrt{3}$ means $60^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{3}$; slope $\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ means $30^\circ = \tfrac{\pi}{6}$.

:::trap Check the calculator mode
When you compute $\sin$, $\cos$, or $\tan$ of an angle, make sure Desmos (wrench icon → Radians/Degrees) or your calculator is set to the unit the question uses.
:::

## Summary

- $180^\circ = \pi$ radians; multiply by $\tfrac{\pi}{180}$ or $\tfrac{180}{\pi}$.
- Arc length in radians: $s = r\theta$.
- Set your calculator to the right angle mode.
