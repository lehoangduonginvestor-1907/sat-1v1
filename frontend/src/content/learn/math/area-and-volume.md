Area and Volume questions give you a shape and ask for an area, perimeter, surface area, or volume, or for how a change in dimensions affects them. The reference sheet lists the formulas, but the questions reward knowing how to use them quickly.

## Two-dimensional shapes

| Shape | Area | Perimeter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rectangle | $\ell w$ | $2\ell + 2w$ |
| square | $s^2$ | $4s$ |
| parallelogram | $bh$ (perpendicular height) | sum of sides |
| triangle | $\tfrac{1}{2}bh$ | sum of sides |
| trapezoid | $\tfrac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)h$ | sum of sides |
| circle | $\pi r^2$ | $2\pi r$ |

For irregular figures, split into rectangles and triangles, or subtract a cut-out piece from a larger shape.

## Prisms and cylinders

A **right prism** or **cylinder** has the same cross-section all the way through:

$$V = (\text{area of base}) \times \text{height}$$

- Rectangular box: $V = \ell wh$; surface area $= 2(\ell w + \ell h + wh)$.
- Cylinder: $V = \pi r^2 h$; surface area $= 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh$ (two circles plus the curved side, which unrolls into a rectangle $2\pi r$ by $h$).
- Cube: $V = s^3$, surface area $= 6s^2$, face diagonal $s\sqrt{2}$.
- Sphere surface area: $4\pi r^2$.

## Spheres, cones, and pyramids

:::rule Pointed solids are one third
$$\text{Cone: } V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h \qquad \text{Pyramid: } V = \frac{1}{3}\ell w h \qquad \text{Sphere: } V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$
:::

A cone has exactly one third the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height; a pyramid is one third of the matching prism.

## Changes that affect area and volume

:::rule Scaling
If every length is multiplied by $k$:
- perimeter (and any length) is multiplied by $k$,
- area (and surface area) by $k^2$,
- volume by $k^3$.
:::

:::examples
✓ Doubling the radius of a sphere multiplies its volume by $2^3 = 8$.
✓ If a cylinder's radius is tripled and its height is halved, the volume is multiplied by $3^2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} = 4.5$. // radius is squared in the formula; height is not.
:::

When only some dimensions change, plug the changes into the formula rather than using the $k^3$ shortcut.

## Similar figures

Similar polygons and solids follow the same scaling rule: if the ratio of corresponding lengths is $a : b$, areas are in ratio $a^2 : b^2$ and volumes in ratio $a^3 : b^3$. Going backwards, if two similar solids have volumes in ratio $8 : 27$, their lengths are in ratio $2 : 3$.

## Density

$$\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$$

Questions combine this with a volume formula: find the volume, then multiply by the density to get the mass (or divide to find a volume). Watch the units, e.g., grams per cubic centimeter.

:::trap Diameter vs. radius
Many questions give the diameter. Halve it before using $\pi r^2$ or $\pi r^2 h$.
:::

:::check
A cylinder has a diameter of 10 cm and a height of 6 cm. What is its volume?
- [ ] $60\pi$ cm³
- [x] $150\pi$ cm³
- [ ] $300\pi$ cm³
- [ ] $600\pi$ cm³
> Radius $= 5$, so $V = \pi(5)^2(6) = 150\pi$.
:::

:::check
Each edge of a cube is increased by 50%. By what factor does the volume increase?
- [ ] $1.5$
- [ ] $2.25$
- [x] $3.375$
- [ ] $4.5$
> Lengths scale by $k = 1.5$, so volume scales by $1.5^3 = 3.375$.
:::

:::check
A cone has radius 3 and height 8. A cylinder has the same radius and height. What is the volume of the cylinder minus the volume of the cone?
- [ ] $24\pi$
- [x] $48\pi$
- [ ] $72\pi$
- [ ] $96\pi$
> Cylinder: $\pi(9)(8) = 72\pi$. Cone: $\tfrac{1}{3}(72\pi) = 24\pi$. Difference: $48\pi$.
:::

## Summary

- Prisms and cylinders: base area × height. Cones and pyramids: one third of that. Sphere: $\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.
- Scale lengths by $k$: area by $k^2$, volume by $k^3$.
- Density = mass ÷ volume.
- Convert diameter to radius first.
