Before the content lessons, it helps to know how the Math section is built and how to use its built-in graphing calculator well. Desmos turns many questions into a few keystrokes, but the test is also designed so that calculator tricks alone won't carry you to a top score.

## How the Math section works

- Two modules of **22 questions in 35 minutes** each. Most questions are multiple choice; about a quarter are student-produced responses (you type the answer).
- The test is **adaptive**: how you do on Module 1 decides whether Module 2 is the harder or easier version. Doing well on the harder Module 2 is the only way to reach the highest scores, so Module 1 matters a lot. Careless mistakes on early, easy questions are expensive.
- Within a module, questions get harder roughly from start to finish.
- A couple of questions in each module are unscored experimental questions, but you can't tell which, so treat every question as real.
- There's no penalty for wrong answers: **never leave a question blank**.

## Using Desmos efficiently

Desmos is built into the test. These are the moves worth practicing until they're automatic:

:::steps
1. **Type, don't click.** Use the keyboard: `^` for exponents, `/` for fractions, `*` to multiply, `sqrt` for a square root, `<=` and `>=` for ≤ and ≥. Arrow keys move you out of an exponent or denominator.
2. **Solve one-variable equations directly.** Type the equation as given, e.g. `x^2+3x-4=0`. Desmos draws vertical lines at the solutions ($x = -4$ and $x = 1$).
3. **Click the gray points.** Desmos marks intersections, intercepts, and maximum/minimum points; clicking shows their coordinates. This solves systems and finds vertices instantly.
4. **Type equations in any form.** Circles like `x^2+y^2-6x+8y=24` and lines like `3x-2y=5` don't need to be rearranged.
5. **Convert decimals to fractions** with the fraction toggle on the left of an output line, useful when the answer choices are fractions.
6. **Define functions and transform them.** Type `f(x)=x^2-4x`, then `f(3)` to evaluate or `g(x)=f(x-3)+2` to see a shift.
7. **Check the angle mode** (wrench icon) before any trigonometry: radians or degrees.
8. **Test equivalent expressions** by graphing the original and a choice: equivalent expressions give the identical graph.
:::

## What Desmos can't do for you

The test writers know about Desmos. Expect many questions where it doesn't help much:

- **Unknown constants**: equations with letters like $a$, $b$, $k$ can't be graphed without values (although a slider sometimes helps).
- **Interpretation questions**: what a slope means in context, which form of an equation shows a feature, what a study can conclude.
- **Geometry reasoning**: similar triangles, angle relationships, congruence.
- **Student-produced responses**: no answer choices to test.

So the goal is to know the math well enough that simple questions take seconds, and to keep Desmos for checking work and for the questions it handles best.

## How to study with this course

- When a practice question stumps you, struggle with it for a few minutes before reading the explanation. That effort is what makes the idea stick.
- Keep an **error log**: every question you miss or guess on gets revisited a few days later until you can solve it cleanly. (The practice page's "Redo My Mistakes" does this for you.)
- Practice the hardest versions of each question type too, since the real test's hardest questions tend to be tougher than the earliest practice tests.
