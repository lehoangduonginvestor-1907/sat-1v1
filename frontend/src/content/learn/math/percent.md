Percentages are their own skill in the Problem-Solving and Data Analysis domain. The questions are usually short, but they're designed to catch one particular mistake: adding and subtracting percents when you should be multiplying.

## Percent means "per hundred"

$$p\% = \frac{p}{100} \qquad \text{so} \qquad 35\% \text{ of } 80 = 0.35 \times 80 = 28$$

Translate words directly into an equation: "of" means multiply, "is" means equals, "what" is the unknown.

"18 is what percent of 72?" → $18 = \tfrac{p}{100} \cdot 72$ → $p = 25$.

## Multiplication factors

:::rule Percent change as a multiplier
- An **increase** of $p\%$ multiplies by $\left(1 + \tfrac{p}{100}\right)$. A 15% increase: $\times 1.15$.
- A **decrease** of $p\%$ multiplies by $\left(1 - \tfrac{p}{100}\right)$. A 15% decrease: $\times 0.85$.
:::

This is the single most useful idea in the topic, because successive changes simply **multiply**:

$$\text{20\% increase, then 20\% decrease: } \; 1.20 \times 0.80 = 0.96$$

The result is a 4% **decrease** overall, not "back to the start." Chain every step into one product instead of computing intermediate prices: a 20% markup, then 25% off, then 8% tax gives $1.20 \times 0.75 \times 1.08 = 0.972$, so the final price is 97.2% of the original.

:::trap Percents don't add
- Two successive 10% increases are $1.1 \times 1.1 = 1.21$: a 21% increase, not 20%.
- A 25% discount followed by another 10% off is $0.75 \times 0.9 = 0.675$: 32.5% off, not 35%.
:::

## Working backwards

If you know the value **after** a change, divide by the multiplier to get the original.

:::examples
✓ After a 20% markup, a jacket costs \$84. Original price: $84 \div 1.2 = 70$. // not $84 \times 0.8 = 67.20$.
:::

## The percent change formula

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%$$

Always divide by the **original** (old) value. From 40 to 50 is a 25% increase; from 50 to 40 is a 20% decrease.

Reading a multiplier in reverse is just as important: $0.4x$ is **60% less** than $x$, and $2.5x$ is **150% greater** than $x$ (because $2.5 = 1 + 1.5$).

:::trap Reversing a change doesn't use the same percent
If enrollment dropped 20% from one year to the next, the earlier year was not 20% higher. If the new value is $k$, the old value is $\tfrac{k}{0.8} = 1.25k$: **25%** higher.
:::

## "Percent of" vs. "percent greater than"

- "$A$ is 30% greater than $B$" → $A = 1.3B$. The quantity after **"than"** is the base you divide by: percent greater $= \tfrac{A - B}{B} \times 100\%$.
- If $A = 200$ and $B = 50$, then $A$ is **400% of** $B$ but **300% greater than** $B$. These two phrasings differ by exactly 100%.
- "$x$ is 40% of $y$, and $y$ is 25% of $z$" → $x = 0.4(0.25z) = 0.1z$, so $x$ is 10% of $z$.

When a question gives no actual numbers, pick a convenient starting value such as 100.

:::check
The price of a bicycle was increased by 10% and then the new price was decreased by 10%. The final price is what percent of the original price?
- [ ] $90\%$
- [x] $99\%$
- [ ] $100\%$
- [ ] $101\%$
> Multiply the factors: $1.10 \times 0.90 = 0.99$, so the final price is 99% of the original.
:::

:::check
After a 35% decrease, the population of a town was 5,200. What was the population before the decrease?
- [ ] 3,380
- [ ] 7,020
- [x] 8,000
- [ ] 8,600
> The new value is $0.65 \times$ the original, so the original is $5{,}200 \div 0.65 = 8{,}000$.
:::

:::check
A number $x$ is 150% of $y$. Which equation represents this relationship?
- [ ] $x = 0.15y$
- [ ] $x = 1.05y$
- [x] $x = 1.5y$
- [ ] $x = 150y$
> 150% = 1.5, and "of" means multiply: $x = 1.5y$.
:::

## Summary

- "Of" = multiply; convert percents to decimals.
- Increase $p\%$: $\times(1 + p/100)$; decrease: $\times(1 - p/100)$; successive changes multiply.
- To undo a change, divide by the multiplier.
- Percent change divides by the original value.
