Many Problem-Solving and Data Analysis questions are really reading questions: the answer is in a table, bar graph, or line graph, and the challenge is extracting exactly the right number. Most errors here come from reading the wrong row, column, or scale.

## Before you read any numbers

:::steps
1. Read the **title** and **axis labels**: what is measured, and in what units?
2. Check the **scale**: does each gridline mean 1, 5, 10, or 1,000? Does the axis start at zero?
3. Read the **legend** if there are several data series.
4. Reread the question and locate the exact row/column or bar it asks about.
:::

## Tables

Two-way tables (rows and columns with totals) are the most common. Questions ask for a cell, a row or column total, or a fraction of one of them.

| | Prefers tea | Prefers coffee | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Students | 48 | 32 | 80 |
| Teachers | 6 | 14 | 20 |
| Total | 54 | 46 | 100 |

"What fraction of **teachers** prefer coffee?" → $\tfrac{14}{20}$. "What fraction of people who prefer coffee are teachers?" → $\tfrac{14}{46}$. The wording decides the denominator.

For "neither" or "not in A or B" questions, either add up the remaining categories or subtract A and B from the total; both give the same count.

## Bar graphs and line graphs

- Read bar heights against the axis, estimating carefully between gridlines.
- On a line graph, the **steepest** segment shows the greatest rate of change; a segment going down is a decrease.
- "Between which two consecutive years did the value increase the most?" → compare segment rises, not the heights themselves.

## Interpreting rate of change from a graph

The slope of a segment between two points is the average rate of change over that interval, in (y-units per x-unit). A horizontal segment means no change.

:::trap "Increase" vs. "percent increase"
The biggest **amount** of increase isn't always the biggest **percent** increase. Going from 20 to 30 is +10 (50%); going from 100 to 115 is +15 (only 15%).
:::

## Scatterplots and lines of best fit

Data points with a trend line appear here too; reading a predicted value means reading the **line**, not a data point. (Details in Statistics II.)

## Summary

- Read the title, axes, scale, and legend before any numbers.
- In two-way tables, the phrase after "of" sets the denominator.
- Steepest segment = greatest rate of change; amount of change ≠ percent change.
