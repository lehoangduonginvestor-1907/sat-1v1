Angle questions belong to the Lines, Angles, and Triangles skill. A small set of facts solves nearly all of them; the work is spotting which fact applies.

## Basic angle facts

:::rule Angle relationships
- Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$ (supplementary).
- Angles around a point add to $360^\circ$.
- Vertical angles (opposite each other where two lines cross) are equal.
- Complementary angles add to $90^\circ$.
- The angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$.
:::

## Exterior angle theorem

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the **sum of the two remote interior angles** (the two angles not next to it).

$$\text{exterior angle} = a + b$$

This saves a step: you don't need to find the third interior angle first.

## Parallel lines cut by a transversal

When a line crosses two parallel lines, it creates 8 angles of only **two sizes**: a "big" angle and a "small" angle that add to $180^\circ$.

- **Corresponding** angles (same position at each intersection) are equal.
- **Alternate interior** angles (inside the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal) are equal: the "Z" shape.
- **Alternate exterior** angles (outside the parallel lines, on opposite sides) are equal.
- **Same-side interior** angles add to $180^\circ$: the "C" or "U" shape.

These work in reverse too: if corresponding (or alternate interior) angles are equal, the two lines **must** be parallel.

:::tip The big/small shortcut
In a parallel-lines diagram, every acute angle is equal to every other acute angle, and every obtuse angle equals every other obtuse angle. Any acute + any obtuse = $180^\circ$.
:::

:::trap Only if the lines are parallel
These equalities hold only when the problem states (or marks) that the lines are parallel. Diagrams are drawn to scale unless noted, but never assume parallel lines from appearance alone when a question hinges on it.
:::

## Polygons

The interior angles of an $n$-sided polygon add to

$$(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$$

because the polygon can be split into $n - 2$ triangles. A **regular** polygon has equal angles, so each interior angle is $\dfrac{(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ}{n}$. The exterior angles of any polygon (one at each vertex) always add to $360^\circ$.

| Polygon | Sides | Interior angle sum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| triangle | 3 | $180^\circ$ |
| quadrilateral | 4 | $360^\circ$ |
| pentagon | 5 | $540^\circ$ |
| hexagon | 6 | $720^\circ$ |

:::check
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One of the angles formed measures $68^\circ$. Which of the following could NOT be the measure of another angle formed?
- [ ] $68^\circ$
- [ ] $112^\circ$
- [x] $122^\circ$
- [ ] All of these angles are possible.
> Only two sizes occur: $68^\circ$ and $180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$. So $122^\circ$ is impossible.
:::

:::check
In a triangle, two interior angles measure $47^\circ$ and $61^\circ$. What is the measure of the exterior angle adjacent to the third angle?
- [ ] $72^\circ$
- [x] $108^\circ$
- [ ] $119^\circ$
- [ ] $133^\circ$
> By the exterior angle theorem, it equals the sum of the remote interior angles: $47^\circ + 61^\circ = 108^\circ$.
:::

:::check
What is the measure of each interior angle of a regular octagon (8 sides)?
- [ ] $120^\circ$
- [x] $135^\circ$
- [ ] $140^\circ$
- [ ] $1{,}080^\circ$
> Sum $= (8 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 1{,}080^\circ$; each of 8 equal angles is $1{,}080^\circ \div 8 = 135^\circ$.
:::

## Summary

- Straight line 180°, around a point 360°, vertical angles equal, triangle 180°.
- Exterior angle = sum of the two remote interior angles.
- Parallel lines: only two angle sizes, which add to 180°.
- Polygon interior sum $(n - 2) \cdot 180^\circ$; exterior angles sum to 360°.
