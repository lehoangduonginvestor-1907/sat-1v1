Text completion questions end with a blank and ask "Which choice most logically completes the text?" They belong to the Inferences skill, and they make up a good share of the harder Reading questions. The good news: they're really literal comprehension questions with one small extra step. Everything you need is in the passage; you just have to state the conclusion the passage is heading toward.

## The method

The key information is usually in the sentence **right before the blank**. Earlier sentences tend to give background.

:::steps
1. **Read the claim** that leads into the blank (usually the last full sentence).
2. **Restate it** in 3–6 words of your own. Write it down if the passage is dense.
3. **Work out the implication**, staying as close to the text as possible. If your conclusion feels almost too obvious, that's a good sign.
4. **Find the choice that matches**, even if it uses very different words.
:::

:::examples
✓ Passage: Lizards on the windiest islands have longer front legs, which help them grip branches. After a hurricane, researchers found that the surviving lizards had longer front legs than the island average. Therefore, ____ → "front-leg length likely affected which lizards survived the storm." // Restate: longer legs → better grip → more likely to survive.
✗ Same passage → "hurricanes are becoming more frequent in the region." // Could be true in real life, but the passage says nothing about frequency.
:::

## Watch the word before the blank

The transition that introduces the blank tells you the relationship you need:

- *therefore, thus, so, as a result* → a conclusion that follows from what came before;
- *for example* → a specific case of the previous claim;
- *however, but, yet* → something that goes against the expectation just set up;
- *whereas, while* → a contrast within the sentence.

## Valid vs. invalid conclusions

Wrong answers on completions are often tempting because they mention people or things from the passage. Learn to sort them.

**Valid inferences** restate what the passage implies from another angle:
- If a method is "the *most* effective," then other methods are *less* effective.
- If a behavior occurs "*only* at night," then it doesn't occur during the day.
- If researchers "*largely* focused on adults," then children were studied less.
- Double negatives: "not uncommon" = common; "not impossible" = possible.

**Invalid inferences** go beyond the text:
- **Speculation**: it *could* be true, but the passage gives no basis for it.
- **Real-world facts**: true in reality, but not stated or implied in the text.
- **Judgments of effectiveness**: the passage says a method is *used*; the answer says it's the *best*.
- **New times, places, or groups**: the passage covers 2000–2020; the answer talks about the 1800s.
- **Unexplained reasons**: the answer explains *why* something happened when the passage never says.

:::trap Don't add a step
The correct completion needs only one small logical step. If a choice requires you to imagine an extra fact ("…so researchers will probably ask for more funding"), it's wrong.
:::

:::check
"Most seeds need light to germinate, but the seeds of one desert shrub germinate only after they have been exposed to smoke from wildfires. Because fires clear away competing plants and release nutrients into the soil, this trait likely means that the shrub's seedlings ____"
- [ ] grow faster in shady conditions than in sunlight.
- [x] begin growing at a time when they face less competition for resources.
- [ ] are more likely to be destroyed by later fires.
- [ ] need more water than seedlings of other desert plants.
> Restate: smoke triggers germination; fires clear competitors and add nutrients. So the seedlings start growing right when competition is low. The other choices introduce ideas (shade, later fires, water) the passage never mentions.
:::

:::check
"A survey asked shoppers why they chose locally grown vegetables. Almost none mentioned taste or price; instead, most said they wanted to support farmers in their region. This suggests that for many of these shoppers, ____"
- [ ] local vegetables are cheaper than imported ones.
- [ ] taste is the most important factor in choosing produce.
- [x] buying local produce is motivated more by community concerns than by the qualities of the food itself.
- [ ] farmers should advertise the flavor of their vegetables.
> Shoppers cited supporting regional farmers, not taste or price. C states that directly in general terms. A and B contradict the survey; D makes a recommendation the passage doesn't imply.
:::

:::check
"Until recently, astronomers believed that the planet's thin rings were billions of years old. However, new measurements show that the rings are made of ice so clean that it could not have been exposed to dust from space for very long. Thus, the findings suggest that ____"
- [ ] the planet itself is younger than astronomers thought.
- [x] the rings may have formed much more recently than previously believed.
- [ ] the rings will disappear within a few years.
- [ ] dust from space is more common near the planet than elsewhere.
> Clean ice means little exposure to dust, which means the rings haven't been around long. That contradicts the "billions of years" belief. A changes the topic from the rings to the planet; C and D add claims without support.
:::

## Summary

- The key sentence is usually right before the blank; restate it, then take one small step.
- The transition before the blank tells you the relationship.
- Valid: restating from another angle, negations, double negatives.
- Invalid: speculation, real-world facts, effectiveness claims, new times/places, invented reasons.
