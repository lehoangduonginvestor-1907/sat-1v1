Literal comprehension questions ask what the passage directly says: "According to the text, what is true about the study?" or "Based on the text, why did the team change its approach?" You'll usually see one or two per module. They're the most straightforward Reading questions, but they have a twist: the correct answer never uses the passage's exact words.

## Same idea, different words

The test checks whether you understand an idea well enough to recognize it **paraphrased**, usually in more general or abstract language.

| The passage says | The correct answer might say |
| --- | --- |
| "the newsletter was mailed to subscribers in forty countries" | "it had an international readership" |
| "farmers planted beans between the rows of corn so that the beans would enrich the soil" | "they used one crop to benefit another" |
| "the longer the drought lasted, the harder it became for the trees to recover" | "recovery grew more difficult over time" |

Think of these questions as a matching game: find the lines, then find the choice that says the same thing in other words.

## Find the exact lines and read the whole sentence

:::steps
1. Identify what the question asks about (the study, a character, a reason).
2. Scan for where the passage discusses it.
3. Read the **entire sentence** containing it, and the sentence before if needed.
4. Restate that information in your own words, then match.
:::

:::trap The half-sentence trap
Suppose a passage says: "Although the survey included only residents of the capital, its authors argue that the trend probably holds across the whole country." A question asks what is true about the survey. If you start reading at "its authors argue," you'll miss the key fact: the survey **did not include anyone outside the capital**. The answer is often in the part of the sentence you skipped.
:::

## Why wrong answers are wrong

- **Contradicted**: says the opposite of the passage.
- **Not mentioned**: plausible, but the passage never says it (e.g., the passage says a newsletter had readers in many countries, not that it was the *most popular* newsletter).
- **Comparison not made**: claims something is *more* or *less* than something else when the passage makes no comparison.
- **Too extreme**: turns "enjoyed" into "was obsessed with," or "some effect" into "a dramatic effect."
- **Wrong part of the passage**: an accurate detail that answers a different question.

:::tip Unknown words in the choices
You don't need to know every word in every choice. If one choice clearly matches the passage, you can pick it without worrying about the vocabulary in the others.
:::

:::check
"The city's first public library opened in 1897 with just 3,000 books. Its founders, a group of local schoolteachers, raised the money by organizing concerts and bake sales over five years." According to the text, how was the library funded?
- [ ] by a grant from the city government
- [x] through community fundraising events led by teachers
- [ ] by charging readers a small fee to borrow books
- [ ] through donations of books from other libraries
> "Concerts and bake sales" organized by schoolteachers = community fundraising events led by teachers.
:::

:::check
"Unlike most frogs, which lay hundreds of eggs and leave them unattended, the strawberry poison frog lays only a few eggs and carries each tadpole on its back to a separate pool of water, returning regularly to feed it." Based on the text, what is true about the strawberry poison frog?
- [ ] It lays more eggs than most frogs.
- [ ] It abandons its eggs soon after laying them.
- [x] It invests unusual care in a small number of offspring.
- [ ] Its tadpoles find their own way to water.
> "Only a few eggs," carrying each tadpole, feeding it: unusual care for a small number of offspring. A and B describe *other* frogs; D contradicts "carries each tadpole."
:::

:::check
"The novelist wrote her first three books in the early morning before going to work at a bank, a habit she kept even after her fourth novel became a bestseller." According to the text, what did the novelist do after her fourth novel's success?
- [ ] She quit her job at the bank.
- [x] She continued writing in the early morning.
- [ ] She stopped writing novels.
- [ ] She began writing in the evenings.
> "A habit she kept" = she continued writing early in the morning. The passage doesn't say whether she quit her job.
:::
