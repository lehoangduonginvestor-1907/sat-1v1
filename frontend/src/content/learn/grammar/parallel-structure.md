Parallel structure means that items doing the same job in a sentence take the same grammatical form. It shows up in lists and in pairs joined by *and*, *or*, or *but*. The SAT doesn't test it as often as punctuation or verbs, but when it does, the fix is usually visible at a glance.

## Lists

:::rule Lists of three or more
Every item in a list must have the same form: all nouns, all *-ing* words, all *to* + verb, all clauses, and so on.
:::

:::examples
✗ The new trail is designed for hiking, cycling, and to watch birds. // -ing, -ing, to + verb.
✓ The new trail is designed for hiking, cycling, and bird-watching.
✗ The volunteers cleared brush, were planting saplings, and built fences.
✓ The volunteers cleared brush, planted saplings, and built fences.
:::

When the whole list is in the choices, compare just the **first word** of each item. If two items begin with *-ing* verbs, the third should too.

Lists separated by semicolons follow the same rule for punctuation: once you use semicolons between items, use them between every item, not a mix of commas and semicolons.

## Pairs

Two items joined by *and*, *or*, *but*, or *than* should match. Look at the part of the sentence that isn't blank, and copy its form.

:::examples
✓ The goal of the program is to **reduce** traffic and **improve** air quality. // Repeating "to" before "improve" is optional.
✗ The goal of the program is to reduce traffic and improving air quality.
✓ The delay was caused by **both** financial **constraints** and an increased **emphasis** on safety. // noun phrase + noun phrase
✗ The delay was caused by both financial constraints and because safety became more important. // noun phrase + clause
:::

## Matching number

When items refer to groups of people or things, keep the nouns in the same number: "Members of the council served as **advisors or ambassadors**," not "an advisor or ambassadors."

## Choosing among correct-sounding options

If several choices are grammatical but only one keeps the pattern, choose it. When choices are equally parallel, the SAT prefers the one that is active and concise.

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? The field guide teaches readers how to identify common mushrooms, how to tell edible species from poisonous ones, and ____
- [ ] safely harvesting them without damaging the surrounding soil.
- [ ] the safe harvesting of them without soil damage.
- [x] how to harvest them without damaging the surrounding soil.
- [ ] they should harvest them without damaging the surrounding soil.
> The first two items both begin with "how to." The third must too.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? For the exhibit, the curator chose paintings that were bold in color, unusual in subject, and ____
- [ ] they were often made by artists who were not well known.
- [x] largely unknown to the public.
- [ ] the artists were largely unknown to the public.
- [ ] having been largely unknown to the public.
> The list is a series of adjective phrases describing the paintings: "bold in color," "unusual in subject," "largely unknown to the public." The other choices switch to full clauses or an -ing phrase.
:::
