Rhetorical Synthesis questions are the ones with bulleted notes: "While researching a topic, a student has taken the following notes." The question then says what the student wants to do (*emphasize a difference*, *introduce a study to an unfamiliar audience*, *explain an advantage*), and you pick the sentence that does it. They usually appear as the last few questions of a Reading and Writing module.

These questions look long, but they are often the fastest points on the test once you see the trick.

## The trick: the question tells you the answer

Every answer choice is built from true information in the notes. So the notes cannot tell you which choice is right. The **goal in the question** can.

:::steps
1. Skip the notes at first. Read the question and underline the goal: *what* the student wants to do and *for whom*.
2. Turn the goal into a checklist. "Emphasize a similarity between the two paintings" means the answer must (a) mention both paintings and (b) present something they share.
3. Go through the choices and eliminate any that miss an item on the checklist.
4. Go back to the notes only if two choices survive and you need to confirm a detail.
:::

## Matching the goal precisely

Wrong answers are rarely false. They are **off-goal**: they talk about the right topic but do a different job.

| If the goal is to… | The correct choice will… | A typical wrong choice… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| emphasize a **similarity** | name both items and use words like *both*, *also*, *like*, *similarly*, *share* | describes each item separately without connecting them |
| emphasize a **difference** / contrast | use *whereas*, *while*, *unlike*, *however*, *but*, *in contrast* | lists facts about each item with no contrast |
| introduce a **study's aim** or research question | state what the researchers wanted to find out | reports the results instead |
| present a **finding** or conclusion | state the result | describes only the method |
| give an **example** of a broader point | name the general point and a specific case | gives the specific case without the general point (or vice versa) |
| explain a **cause** or an **advantage** | contain because, so, allows, which made it possible… | gives two facts with no link between them |

## Familiar vs. unfamiliar audience

Some questions add an audience: "for an audience **unfamiliar** with the artist" or "for an audience **already familiar** with the study."

- **Unfamiliar audience**: the reader needs background. The answer identifies who or what the subject is ("Nigerian sculptor Ben Enwonwu," "a 2019 study of honeybee navigation by researchers at…"). It is often the longest choice.
- **Familiar audience**: no introduction needed. The answer can use the name alone and jump straight to the point.

:::examples
✓ Goal: introduce the novel to an unfamiliar audience. → *The Moon Ferry*, a 1998 novel by Kenyan writer A. Mwangi, follows a fisherman who ferries strangers across Lake Victoria. // Says what the work is and who wrote it.
✗ Goal: introduce the novel to an unfamiliar audience. → *The Moon Ferry* was praised for its spare, lyrical prose. // Assumes the reader already knows what the book is.
:::

:::trap Close but not exact
The most tempting wrong choice usually satisfies **half** of the goal. If the goal is "emphasize the *duration* of the expedition," a choice that gives the expedition's route and purpose but not its length is wrong, even though it is accurate and on topic. Check every word of the goal.
:::

:::tip
Signal words do a lot of the work. If the goal says "contrast," scan the choices for *whereas*, *while*, *unlike*, *but*; if it says "similarity," scan for *both*, *also*, *like*, *similarly*. Often only one or two choices contain the right kind of word.
:::

## Practice

:::check
Notes: • The Atacama Desert in Chile receives less than 1 mm of rain per year in some areas. • The Namib Desert in southern Africa receives about 2–20 mm per year near the coast. • Both deserts get much of their moisture from coastal fog. The student wants to emphasize a **similarity** between the two deserts. Which choice best accomplishes this goal?
- [ ] The Atacama Desert receives less than 1 mm of rain per year in some areas, while the Namib receives up to 20 mm.
- [x] Both the Atacama and the Namib, though on different continents, draw much of their moisture from coastal fog.
- [ ] The Namib Desert, located in southern Africa, receives about 2–20 mm of rain per year near the coast.
- [ ] Some parts of the Atacama Desert in Chile receive less than 1 mm of rain per year.
> Only B names both deserts and states something they share. A contrasts them; C and D each describe one desert.
:::

:::check
Notes: • In 2021, a team of ecologists studied city foxes in three European capitals. • They wanted to know whether urban foxes have smaller home ranges than rural foxes. • Urban foxes' home ranges were on average 70% smaller. The student wants to present the **aim** of the study. Which choice best accomplishes this goal?
- [ ] Urban foxes in three European capitals had home ranges about 70% smaller than rural foxes.
- [ ] In 2021, ecologists tracked foxes living in three European capitals.
- [x] A 2021 study of foxes in three European capitals set out to determine whether urban foxes occupy smaller home ranges than rural ones.
- [ ] Ecologists have long been interested in how foxes adapt to cities.
> The aim is what the researchers wanted to find out. C states that question. A gives the result, B only the method, and D a vague background claim.
:::

:::check
Notes: • Mary Golda Ross (1908–2008) was an engineer. • She was the first known Native American female engineer. • At Lockheed, she worked on early designs for spacecraft and satellites. The student wants to introduce Mary Golda Ross to an audience **unfamiliar** with her. Which choice best accomplishes this goal?
- [ ] Ross worked on early designs for spacecraft and satellites.
- [x] Mary Golda Ross (1908–2008), the first known Native American female engineer, worked on early spacecraft designs at Lockheed.
- [ ] At Lockheed, early spacecraft and satellite designs were developed.
- [ ] Ross was the first known Native American female engineer.
> An unfamiliar reader needs her full name and who she was. B gives both plus what she did; A and D assume we already know who "Ross" is, and C doesn't mention her at all.
:::

## Summary

- All choices use true facts from the notes, so the notes alone cannot decide.
- Translate the goal into a checklist and eliminate anything that misses part of it.
- Unfamiliar audience → background; familiar audience → no background.
- Watch for the "half-right" choice that matches the topic but not the specific goal.
