This last grammar lesson gathers a few smaller rules: fixed word pairs, comparisons, countable vs. uncountable nouns, and question marks. None of them appears often, but each one is quick to learn and easy to recognize when it does.

## Fixed word pairs

Some words always travel with a specific partner. If you see the first half, the second half must match.

| Pair | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **either … or** | You can register **either** online **or** in person. |
| **neither … nor** | The map was **neither** accurate **nor** complete. |
| **not only … but (also)** | The bridge is **not only** longer **but also** cheaper to maintain. |
| **both … and** | The study measured **both** sleep **and** mood. |
| **as … as** | The new battery lasts **as** long **as** the old one. |
| **more / -er … than**, **less … than** | The canyon is **deeper than** it looks. |
| **between … and** | The vote was split **between** the north **and** the south. |

:::examples
✗ The festival attracts not only local families and also tourists from abroad.
✓ The festival attracts not only local families but also tourists from abroad.
✗ The results were neither surprising or new.
✓ The results were neither surprising nor new.
:::

The items in a pair should also be parallel (see the previous lesson): "**both** *to inform* **and** *to entertain*," not "both to inform and entertainment."

## Comparisons: like with like

A comparison has to compare things of the same kind. The most common error compares a **thing** with a **person**, or a part of something with the whole of something else.

:::examples
✗ The hummingbird's heart rate is far higher than a human. // Heart rate vs. a person.
✓ The hummingbird's heart rate is far higher than **that of** a human. // Heart rate vs. heart rate.
✓ The hummingbird's heart rate is far higher than a **human's**. // "A human's" = a human's heart rate.
✗ Unlike most novels of its time, the author wrote the book in short, unnumbered sections. // The author is compared with novels.
✓ Unlike most novels of its time, the book was written in short, unnumbered sections.
:::

Use **that of** for a singular thing and **those of** for plural things: "The wings of a bat are more flexible than **those of** a bird."

## Countable vs. uncountable

- Things you can count (*cars, mistakes, species*) take **fewer** and **many**: *fewer errors, many species*.
- Things you can't count (*water, information, traffic*) take **less** and **much**: *less traffic, much information*.

## Direct and indirect questions

A **direct question** is a sentence that asks something. It has question word order and ends with a question mark: "How do bees find their way home?"

An **indirect question** reports a question inside a statement. It uses **statement word order** (subject before verb) and ends with a **period**.

:::examples
✓ Researchers wanted to know **how bees find** their way home. // Statement order, period.
✗ Researchers wanted to know how do bees find their way home? // Question order inside a statement.
✓ The team asked **whether the bridge could** be repaired. // "whether/if" + statement order.
✗ The team asked whether could the bridge be repaired.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? The archaeologists hoped to determine ____
- [ ] when was the settlement abandoned?
- [ ] when was the settlement abandoned.
- [x] when the settlement was abandoned.
- [ ] when the settlement was abandoned?
> This is an indirect question inside a statement ("The archaeologists hoped to determine…"), so it uses statement order (subject "the settlement" before "was") and ends with a period.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? The shells of these deep-sea snails contain far more iron than ____
- [ ] most snails.
- [x] those of most snails.
- [ ] that of most snails.
- [ ] most snails do.
> Shells must be compared with shells. "Those of" stands for "the shells of" (plural). A compares shells with snails; C uses the singular "that"; D compares what snails do, not their shells.
:::
