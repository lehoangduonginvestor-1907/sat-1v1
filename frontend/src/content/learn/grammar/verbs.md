Verb questions are the largest group of Form, Structure, and Sense questions. The four choices are usually forms of one verb (*has / have / had / was having*), and they test one of two things:

- **Agreement**: is the verb singular or plural to match its subject?
- **Tense**: does the verb's time fit the rest of the passage?

Look at the choices first to see which one is being tested. If some are singular and some are plural, it's an agreement question: find the subject and ignore tense distractors. If all have the same number but different times, it's a tense question.

## Part 1: Subject–verb agreement

:::rule Agreement
A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.
In the present tense, the singular verb ends in **-s**: *the bird sings / the birds sing*. Also: *is/are*, *was/were*, *has/have*.
:::

That sounds easy. The difficulty is that the SAT always hides the subject. The noun right before the blank is almost never the subject.

### Disguise 1: words between the subject and the verb
Prepositional phrases and non-essential clauses push the subject far away from its verb. Cross them out.

:::examples
✓ The **patent** for the first lightweight bicycle frames **was** filed in 1890. // Cross out "for the first lightweight bicycle frames": the patent was.
✗ The patent for the first lightweight bicycle frames were filed in 1890. // "Frames" is not the subject.
✓ The **diaries**, along with a single photograph of the author, **were** donated to the library. // "Along with…" doesn't make the subject plural or singular; "diaries" decides.
:::

### Disguise 2: the verb inside a *that/which/who* clause
The verb in a relative clause agrees with the noun that the clause describes.

"The museum displays **masks** that **represent** river spirits." (*masks represent*, not *museum represents*.)

### Disguise 3: inverted order
When a sentence starts with a prepositional phrase or *there*, the subject comes **after** the verb.

:::examples
✓ Among the objects found in the tomb **were** two bronze **mirrors**. // Flip it: "two bronze mirrors were among the objects."
✓ There **are** several **reasons** for the delay. // After "there is/are," look at the noun that follows.
✓ Less clear **are** the **effects** of the drug over many years. // The subject "effects" comes last.
:::

### Disguise 4: compound subjects
Two subjects joined by *and* are plural: "The **speed and accuracy** of the new scanner **allow** doctors to…" This is true even in inverted sentences: "Along the riverbank **grow** willows **and** reeds."

With *either…or* / *neither…nor*, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject: "Neither the coach nor the **players were** told."

### Disguise 5: subjects that look plural (or don't)
- A **gerund** (an *-ing* noun) is singular: "**Studying** old maps **helps** historians…"
- A **clause** used as a subject is singular: "**What the survey revealed** **was** surprising." / "**Whether** the bridge can be saved **remains** unclear."
- **Collective nouns** (team, company, committee, audience, group) are treated as singular on the SAT: "The **committee has** approved the plan."
- **Each**, **every**, **one**, **either**, **neither**, **anyone**, **everyone**, **no one** are singular: "**Each** of the paintings **is** signed."
- **Few, both, several, many** are plural.
- **Some, most, all, none, more** depend on the noun after *of*: "Most of the **water is**…" / "Most of the **samples are**…"

## Part 2: Tense

:::rule Tense
Unless the passage clearly signals a change in time, keep verbs consistent with the tense used in the surrounding sentences.
:::

So the most important step is to **read the sentences before and after the blank** and note the tense they use. Many questions are decided by one verb in the previous sentence.

### The tenses and their signals

| Tense | Form | When to use it | Typical signals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| simple past | *studied* | a finished action at a definite time | dates (in 1850), *ago*, *last year* |
| present perfect | *has / have studied* | an action that began in the past and continues, or a recent action with no date | *since*, *for* (a period), *over the past decade*, *in recent years*, *so far* |
| past perfect | *had studied* | the earlier of two past actions | *by the time* + past, *before* + past |
| simple present | *studies* | general truths, ongoing situations, describing what a text or artwork does | present verbs nearby |
| future | *will study* | the future | *next*, *soon*, *by 2040* |
| conditional | *would study* | hypothetical; future seen from a past moment; repeated past habit | *if*, a past-tense frame |

:::examples
✓ **Since** 2015, the city **has planted** over 40,000 trees. // Started in the past, still continuing.
✗ Since 2015, the city planted over 40,000 trees.
✓ **By the time** the rescue team arrived, the storm **had passed**. // The storm passed first.
✓ In 1905, no one could have predicted that the small company **would become** a global brand. // A future event viewed from the past: "would," not "will."
:::

### More tense rules the SAT tests
- **Each verb in a compound needs its own correct form.** "The team **has met** and **will continue** to meet monthly." (Not "has met and continue.")
- **Hypothetical *if* sentences**: "If the dam **had failed**, the valley **would have flooded**." *Would have* never goes in the *if* part.
- **Will have** is the future perfect: "By the time the tunnel opens, workers **will have spent** a decade on it."
- **Irregular participles**: after *has / have / had*, use the participle: *begun, chosen, drawn, flown, frozen, gone, grown, hidden, known, ridden, risen, seen, spoken, stolen, sunk, swum, taken, thrown, written*. The simple past forms (*began, flew, swam, sank*) never follow *has/had*.
- **Prefer the active voice** when nothing else separates the choices; passive versions tend to be wordy.

:::trap Agreement first
When the choices mix singular and plural, don't get distracted by tense. Find the subject, eliminate the wrong number, and only then compare tenses among the survivors.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? The collection of ceramic bowls unearthed near the ancient trading post ____ that the settlement traded with communities hundreds of kilometers away.
- [ ] suggest
- [x] suggests
- [ ] have suggested
- [ ] are suggesting
> The subject is "collection" (singular). "Of ceramic bowls unearthed near the ancient trading post" is just a description. Only "suggests" is singular.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? In the shallow waters off the island ____ a coral species that biologists had believed extinct for decades.
- [ ] live
- [x] lives
- [ ] are living
- [ ] have lived
> The sentence is inverted. Flip it: "a coral species… lives in the shallow waters." The subject "species" is singular here ("a coral species"), so the verb must be singular.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? Over the past two decades, researchers ____ thousands of hours of whale songs, revealing that the songs change gradually from year to year.
- [ ] recorded
- [ ] had recorded
- [x] have recorded
- [ ] will record
> "Over the past two decades" describes a period that started in the past and reaches the present, the signal for the present perfect.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? By the time the first European ships reached the islands, Polynesian navigators ____ them for centuries.
- [ ] inhabit
- [ ] have inhabited
- [x] had inhabited
- [ ] will inhabit
> Two past situations: the ships arriving, and the islands already being inhabited for centuries before that. The earlier, continuing situation takes the past perfect; "by the time" + a past verb is the classic signal.
:::

## Summary

- Singular vs. plural choices → agreement. Find the real subject: cross out phrases and clauses, flip inverted sentences.
- Compound subjects with *and* are plural; gerunds, clauses, collective nouns, and *each/every* are singular.
- Tense: match the surrounding verbs; use *since/over the past* → present perfect, *by the time* + past → past perfect, future-in-the-past → *would*.
