Apostrophe questions look intimidating because the choices pile up apostrophes in different places: *artist's paintings / artists paintings / artist's painting's / artists' paintings'*. But there are only a few rules, and you can usually decide each word separately.

## The four forms of a noun

| Form | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| singular | the **bird** | one bird |
| plural | the **birds** | more than one, **no apostrophe** |
| singular possessive | the **bird's** nest | the nest of one bird |
| plural possessive | the **birds'** nests | the nests of several birds |

Irregular plurals that don't end in *-s* add **'s** for the possessive: *children's*, *people's*, *women's*, *men's*.

An **'s** can also be a contraction of *is* or *has* ("The bird's flown away" = has flown), but the SAT rarely tests nouns this way.

## The shortcut: is the next word a noun?

:::rule Possession test
A noun that is followed directly by **another noun** it owns needs an apostrophe. A noun followed by a verb, a preposition, or anything else does not.
:::

:::examples
✓ The **artist's** brushes were made from horsehair. // "brushes" belongs to the artist → apostrophe.
✓ The **artists** gathered in the studio. // "gathered" is a verb → plain plural, no apostrophe.
✗ The artist's gathered in the studio. // Nothing is owned here.
✗ The artists brushes were made from horsehair. // "brushes" belongs to them → needs an apostrophe.
:::

Then decide **one owner or many** from the meaning. "The **planet's** atmosphere" (one planet). "The **planets'** orbits" (all the planets in the system). Context usually makes it clear: look for *a*, *one*, *each*, numbers, or plural verbs elsewhere in the passage.

## Pronoun possessives have no apostrophe

This is the single most important exception. Possessive pronouns are already possessive, so they never take an apostrophe.

| Possessive (ownership) | Contraction (two words) |
| --- | --- |
| **its** (the cat licked its paw) | **it's** = it is / it has |
| **their** (their research) | **they're** = they are |
| **whose** (whose idea) | **who's** = who is / who has |
| **your** | **you're** = you are |

*Its'* and *its's* don't exist. *There* is a place ("over there"), not a possessive.

:::tip Plug it in
When you see *it's* or *they're*, expand it: "The company changed **it is** logo"? Nonsense → use *its*. "**They are** findings were published"? Nonsense → use *their*.
:::

## Combination questions

Many questions test two things at once: singular vs. plural **and** possessive vs. contraction (*its / it's / their / they're*).

1. First find what the pronoun refers to and decide singular or plural → narrows to *its/it's* or *their/they're*.
2. Then decide possessive or contraction → one answer left.

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? After measuring 200 lizards on the island, researchers found that the ____ were longer in forested areas, which may help the animals climb.
- [ ] lizard's toe's
- [ ] lizards toe's
- [x] lizards' toes
- [ ] lizard's toes
> Many lizards own many toes. "Toes" is a plain plural (followed by the verb "were"), and "lizards'" is a plural possessive because the toes belong to the 200 lizards. "Lizard's" would mean one lizard.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? Although the comet passes near Earth only once every 76 years, ____ path has been predicted with remarkable precision.
- [ ] it's
- [x] its
- [ ] their
- [ ] they're
> The path belongs to the comet (singular) → possessive "its." "It's" would mean "it is path."
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? The two ____ designs for the new library differed in almost every way, from the materials to the layout of the reading rooms.
- [ ] architect's
- [x] architects'
- [ ] architects
- [ ] architects's
> There are two architects, and the designs belong to them: plural possessive "architects'." "Architects's" is never correct.
:::

## Summary

- Plural = no apostrophe. Possessive = apostrophe.
- A noun followed by a noun it owns needs an apostrophe; then choose singular (**'s**) or plural (**s'**) owner.
- Possessive pronouns (*its, their, whose, your*) never take apostrophes; *it's, they're, who's* are contractions.
