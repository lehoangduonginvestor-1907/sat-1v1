Modifier questions are easy to spot: the passage ends with a descriptive phrase and a comma, and the four choices are long, complete-sounding endings that start with different words. They feel like "which sentence sounds best?" questions, but there's a precise rule, and it lets you answer most of them by reading only the **first few words** of each choice.

## Dangling modifiers

A **modifier** is a word or phrase that describes something. When a sentence opens with a descriptive phrase followed by a comma, that phrase describes **whatever noun comes right after the comma**.

:::rule Introductory modifiers
An introductory descriptive phrase must be followed **immediately** by the noun it describes.
:::

:::examples
✗ Built entirely from recycled steel, visitors are amazed by the pavilion. // This says the visitors were built from recycled steel.
✓ Built entirely from recycled steel, the pavilion amazes visitors. // The pavilion was built from steel.
✗ After studying the samples for months, the conclusion was clear to the researchers. // A conclusion can't study samples.
✓ After studying the samples for months, the researchers reached a clear conclusion.
:::

When the modifier "dangles" (has no correct noun to attach to), the sentence is wrong even if every word in it is grammatical.

:::steps
1. Find the introductory phrase that ends in a comma (often it starts with an *-ing* or *-ed* word, *after/while/when* + *-ing*, or a noun phrase like "A pioneer of modern dance,").
2. Ask: **who or what** is being described? ("Who was built from recycled steel?" → the pavilion.)
3. Look only at the first word or two of each choice. Keep the one that starts with that noun.
:::

### Possessives are usually wrong
A choice that starts with a possessive (*the scientist's*, *Rivera's*) is almost always a trap. "Raised in a small fishing town, **Rivera's** paintings often show the sea" means the paintings were raised in a fishing town. The noun right after the comma is *paintings*, not *Rivera*.

:::examples
✗ A pioneer of radio astronomy, Grote Reber's backyard telescope detected signals from the Milky Way. // The telescope wasn't a pioneer.
✓ A pioneer of radio astronomy, Grote Reber detected signals from the Milky Way with a telescope he built in his backyard.
:::

A descriptive **title** directly in front of a name is fine and doesn't count as a modifier error: "Dutch engineer **Cornelis Lely** designed the dike."

## Misplaced modifiers inside the sentence

Descriptions anywhere in a sentence should sit next to what they describe. The SAT tests this most often with:

- ***which***: it refers to the noun right before it. "She photographed the bridge from a boat, **which** was painted bright red" suggests the boat was red. If the bridge was red, the description must move.
- **renaming phrases after a comma or in parentheses**: "The festival began in 1871, **a year when** the city was rebuilding" works because *a year* renames *1871*. "The festival began in Paris, **a year when**…" doesn't; *Paris* is a place, not a year. Look at the **end** of the choices for these.

## The scope of these questions

Modifier questions involve only their own sentence. You don't need to read the rest of the passage for logic; you just need to know who is doing or being described.

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? Combining her interests in botany and the new technology of photography, ____
- [ ] Anna Atkins's cyanotypes of algae are considered the first book illustrated with photographs.
- [ ] the first book illustrated with photographs was made from Anna Atkins's cyanotypes of algae.
- [x] Anna Atkins produced cyanotypes of algae that became the first book illustrated with photographs.
- [ ] cyanotypes of algae were produced by Anna Atkins for the first book illustrated with photographs.
> Who combined her interests? Anna Atkins herself, so her name must come right after the comma. A starts with a possessive (the cyanotypes would be doing the combining), and B and D start with the book and the cyanotypes.
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? Stretching nearly 2,300 kilometers along the coast of Australia, ____
- [x] the Great Barrier Reef is the largest coral reef system in the world.
- [ ] scientists consider the Great Barrier Reef the largest coral reef system in the world.
- [ ] the world's largest coral reef system, according to scientists, is located in Australia, called the Great Barrier Reef.
- [ ] it is the Great Barrier Reef that is the largest coral reef system in the world.
> What stretches 2,300 kilometers? The reef. Only A puts "the Great Barrier Reef" right after the comma. B makes scientists stretch along the coast; D starts with a vague "it."
:::

:::check
Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English? After analyzing tree rings from more than 200 ancient pines, ____
- [ ] the region's droughts were found to have lasted longer than previously thought.
- [ ] the length of the region's droughts surprised the climatologists.
- [x] the climatologists concluded that the region's droughts had lasted longer than previously thought.
- [ ] the climatologists' conclusion was that the region's droughts had lasted longer than previously thought.
> Who analyzed the tree rings? The climatologists. D starts with a possessive ("climatologists' conclusion"), which makes the conclusion do the analyzing.
:::

## Summary

- An introductory descriptive phrase + comma must be followed by the noun it describes.
- Ask "who or what is being described?" and check only the first words of each choice.
- A possessive right after the comma is almost always wrong.
- *Which* and renaming phrases describe the noun right before them.
